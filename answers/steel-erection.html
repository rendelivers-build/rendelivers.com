When is fall protection required in steel erection?

Short answer: In steel erection the trigger is 15 feet, not 6. Under 29 CFR 1926.760(a), anyone on a walking/working surface more than 15 feet above a lower level needs guardrails, a safety net, a personal fall arrest system, a positioning system, or a fall restraint system. Connectors between 15 and 30 feet must be given the gear and wear it, with a narrow tie-off exception — and above two stories or 30 feet, everyone ties off, no exceptions.

Subpart R (29 CFR 1926.750–761) is OSHA's steel erection standard. It runs in job order: written permission to start, hoisting and rigging, structural stability, connections, decking, fall protection, and training. It is stricter and more specific than the general construction rules, and inspectors on a steel job know the difference.

Before the first pick — written go-ahead: 1926.752

No steel until the concrete is proven. The controlling contractor must notify the steel erector in writing that footings, piers, and walls have reached 75 percent of design strength (by ASTM field-cured sample tests) or enough strength to support erection loads. Verbal assurance does not satisfy the rule.

The site is part of the rule. The controlling contractor provides adequate access roads into and through the site and a firm, graded, drained laydown and erection area, and must give written notice of any anchor rod that was repaired, replaced, or field-modified. A site-specific erection plan developed by a qualified person covers the sequence, stability, and fall protection — and stays available at the worksite.

Hoisting and rigging — pre-planned, nobody underneath: 1926.753

Every lift is planned before it happens. Load routes are pre-planned so no one has to work under a suspended load — the only exceptions are the connectors making the initial connection and the crew hooking or unhooking the load. A competent person visually inspects the crane before each shift, and a qualified rigger inspects the rigging before each shift.

Multiple lifts are capped hard. A multiple lift rigs a maximum of five members, spaced at least 7 feet apart, rigged from the top down at their center of gravity so they hang balanced and level — and only under a multiple-lift plan by a qualified person, within the crane's rated capacity.

Stability and column anchorage — four rods, 300 pounds: 1926.754, 1926.755

Four anchor rods minimum per column. Each column anchor assembly — rods, base-plate weld, and foundation — must resist a 300-pound eccentric gravity load applied 18 inches from the column face at the top of the shaft. Anchor rods are never repaired, replaced, or field-modified without the project structural engineer of record's approval.

Never outrun the bolting. At the end of a shift there may be no more than four floors or 48 feet of unfinished bolting or welding, whichever is less, and the structure's stability must be maintained at every stage — including while decking is laid tightly and secured as it goes.

Beams and columns — two bolts before the line comes off: 1926.756

Two bolts, wrench-tight, then release. A solid web member stays on the hoisting line until each connection carries at least two bolts of the size and strength on the erection drawings, drawn up wrench-tight (or the engineer of record's equivalent). A competent person decides whether a cantilevered member needs more. Diagonal bracing needs at least one wrench-tight bolt per connection.

Double connections keep one bolt home. Where two members share common holes on opposite sides of a column web, at least one bolt with its wrench-tight nut stays in the first member while the second is landed — unless a shop- or field-attached seat or equivalent device, designed for the load and bolted or welded to both members first, secures it. Come-alongs and half-pins are not a substitute.

Perimeter columns set up the next tier's protection. They must extend at least 48 inches above the finished floor, with holes or devices at 42–45 inches and at the midpoint, so perimeter safety cables can be installed before the next tier goes up.

Fall protection — 15 feet, connectors, and the decking zone: 1926.760

The general rule at 15 feet. Guardrails, safety nets, personal fall arrest, positioning, or fall restraint — one of those systems protects every steel worker more than 15 feet above a lower level. Equipment criteria follow 1926.502, the same performance rules as Subpart M.

Connectors, 15 to 30 feet. The employer must provide a full personal fall arrest system and the connector must wear it and have a tie-off available — but between 15 feet and two stories or 30 feet (whichever is less), the connector is not required to tie off where the employer demonstrates tie-off is infeasible or creates a greater hazard at that connection. Above two stories or 30 feet, that exception ends and conventional fall protection is mandatory for connectors too.

Controlled decking zone (CDZ). Initial metal decking at the leading edge may run inside a marked CDZ between 15 and 30 feet: access limited to the decking crew only, the zone no larger than 90 feet wide by 90 feet deep from the leading edge, and no more than 3,000 square feet of unsecured decking inside it. Perimeter safety cables at the 42-inch top level and the midpoint go up as the structure rises. Training for all of it — connectors, CDZ work, and fall protection — is required under 1926.761, by a qualified person.

Common questions

Why is steel erection 15 feet when general construction is 6 feet?
Subpart R sets its own trigger because connectors work on narrow members where Subpart M's 6-foot systems were negotiated into a trade-specific scheme: full systems at 15 feet, a limited connector exception from 15 to 30 feet, and no exception above 30 feet. Outside steel erection activities, the 6-foot Subpart M rule still applies.

Can erection start on a verbal "the concrete is good"?
No. Under 1926.752 the controlling contractor's notice must be in writing and based on ASTM tests of field-cured samples showing 75 percent of design strength or sufficient strength for erection loads.

Does the two-bolt rule apply to diagonal bracing?
No — bracing has its own line in 1926.756(b): at least one bolt per connection, drawn up wrench-tight. The two-bolt rule in 1926.756(a)(1) governs solid web structural members during final placing.
