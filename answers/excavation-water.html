Can you work in an excavation with water in it?

Short answer: Under 29 CFR 1926.651(h)(1), employees shall not work in excavations with accumulated water, or where water is accumulating, unless adequate precautions have been taken — special support or shield systems, water removal to control the level, or a safety harness and lifeline. Before the dig even opens, 1926.651(a) requires every surface encumbrance located so as to create a hazard to be removed or supported. And any water removal equipment must be monitored by a competent person under 1926.651(h)(2).

Before digging — surface encumbrances: 1926.651(a)

Remove it or support it. Paragraph (a) is one sentence: all surface encumbrances that are located so as to create a hazard to employees shall be removed or supported, as necessary, to safeguard employees. Anything at the surface that could fall, roll, or collapse into the work — or onto the crew — is dealt with first, not after someone is in the hole.

The water rule — no work in water without precautions: 1926.651(h)(1)

Accumulated or accumulating. Paragraph (h)(1) covers both standing water and water that is still coming in. Work is barred unless adequate precautions protect employees against the hazards posed by water accumulation. The standard says the precautions vary with each situation, and names three examples: special support or shield systems to protect from cave-ins, water removal to control the level of accumulating water, or use of a safety harness and lifeline.

Pumps need a competent person watching: 1926.651(h)(2)

Monitored, not just running. Paragraph (h)(2): if water is controlled or prevented from accumulating by the use of water removal equipment, the water removal equipment and operations shall be monitored by a competent person to ensure proper operation. A pump set in the morning and forgotten does not meet it — someone qualified has to watch that it keeps working.

Divert surface water, and inspect after heavy rain: 1926.651(h)(3)

Interrupted drainage. Paragraph (h)(3): if excavation work interrupts the natural drainage of surface water (such as streams), diversion ditches, dikes, or other suitable means shall be used to prevent surface water from entering the excavation and to provide adequate drainage of the area adjacent to the excavation.

Heavy rain. The same paragraph adds: excavations subject to runoff from heavy rains will require an inspection by a competent person and compliance with paragraphs (h)(1) and (h)(2). That sits alongside the general inspection duty at 1926.651(k) — daily, and after every rainstorm or other hazard-increasing occurrence.

Common questions

Is a little standing water automatically a violation?
Paragraph (h)(1) bars work in accumulated or accumulating water unless adequate precautions have been taken. The question is never just how much water — it is whether one of the precautions (support/shield systems, water removal, harness and lifeline, as the situation requires) is actually in place.

Can any worker monitor the pump?
No. Paragraph (h)(2) assigns monitoring of water removal equipment and operations to a competent person, to ensure proper operation.

Does the rain inspection replace the daily inspection?
No. Paragraph (h)(3) requires a competent-person inspection for excavations subject to runoff from heavy rains, on top of the daily and after-every-rainstorm inspections at 1926.651(k)(1).
