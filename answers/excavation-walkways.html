How do you get in, out, and across an excavation?

Home Apparel Tools Catalog Safety Gear Shop by Trade Templates Software & Literature Books Gift Guide

Short answer: Under 29 CFR 1926.651(l), walkways shall be provided wherever employees or equipment are required or permitted to cross over an excavation, and a walkway 6 feet or more above a lower level needs guardrails that comply with 1926.502(b). Getting out is just as specific: under 1926.651(c)(2), a trench 4 feet or more deep needs a stairway, ladder, ramp, or other safe egress placed so no employee travels more than 25 feet laterally to reach it. And under 1926.651(c)(1)(i), structural ramps are designed by a competent person — equipment ramps by a competent person qualified in structural design.

Crossing over — walkways: 1926.651(l)

Required or permitted means provided. Paragraph (l) is one sentence with two duties: walkways shall be provided where employees or equipment are required or permitted to cross over excavations — nobody balances across a plank that was never meant to be a crossing, and no machine straddles a trench on improvised timbers. The second duty is the guardrail trigger below.

Guardrails at 6 feet: 1926.651(l) + 1926.502(b)

Six feet up means railed. Paragraph (l) adds: guardrails which comply with 1926.502(b) shall be provided where walkways are 6 feet (1.8 m) or more above lower levels. That pulls in the full guardrail spec — top rail 42 inches plus or minus 3 inches, midrails, and the 200-pound top-rail strength requirement of the fall protection standard.

Getting out — the 25-foot rule: 1926.651(c)(2)

Four feet deep, 25 feet max. Paragraph (c)(2): a stairway, ladder, ramp or other safe means of egress shall be located in trench excavations that are 4 feet (1.22 m) or more in depth so as to require no more than 25 feet (7.62 m) of lateral travel for employees. In a cave-in there is no time to run the length of the trench — the way out has to already be within 25 feet of wherever the work is.

Ramps are designed, not improvised: 1926.651(c)(1)

Competent person designs them. Paragraph (c)(1)(i): structural ramps used solely by employees shall be designed by a competent person; ramps used for equipment access or egress shall be designed by a competent person qualified in structural design, and constructed in accordance with the design. The build details at (c)(1)(ii)–(v): members of multi-member ramps and runways connected together to prevent displacement; structural members of uniform thickness; connecting cleats attached to the bottom of the runway or otherwise arranged to prevent tripping; and ramps used in lieu of steps given cleats or other surface treatment on top to prevent slipping.

Common questions

Does the 25-foot rule apply to every excavation?
Paragraph (c)(2) states it for trench excavations 4 feet or more in depth. Wider excavations still need safe access and egress under (c)(1) — the 25-foot lateral-travel number is the trench rule.

Can a worker design the equipment ramp?
No. Paragraph (c)(1)(i) requires equipment ramps to be designed by a competent person qualified in structural design, and built in accordance with that design.

Does a low walkway need guardrails?
Paragraph (l) requires guardrails complying with 1926.502(b) where walkways are 6 feet or more above lower levels. Below that height the walkway itself is still required wherever crossing is required or permitted.
